How Much Does Branding Cost for a Small Business in the UK?

OakFox · 13 October 2026
branding brand identity small business uk pricing

Branding prices vary more than almost anything else a small business buys. You can get a logo for the cost of lunch, or spend tens of thousands with an agency, and both get called “branding”. The difference is what you’re actually getting. Here’s how the UK market breaks down in 2026, with our own published prices as a concrete reference point.

The short answer

RouteTypical costWhat you getBest for
Logo marketplace or DIYVery littleA mark, usually without strategy or rules for using itTesting an idea before you commit

Logo or identity: what you’re paying for

A logo is one mark. An identity is the system that makes everything you put out recognisably yours. A full identity usually covers:

  • Positioning: who you’re for, what you stand for, and why someone should choose you.
  • Naming, if the business or product needs a name. It’s its own discipline, which we cover in choosing a brand name.
  • The visual system: logo suite, colour, typography, imagery and illustration direction.
  • Voice and messaging: how you sound, and the handful of lines you’ll use everywhere.
  • Guidelines: the rules that let a printer, a web developer or a new hire apply the brand without guessing.
  • Launch assets: the first real applications, such as social templates, signage, stationery or packaging.

Most of the long-term value sits in the system, not the mark. A great logo applied inconsistently is weaker than a good logo applied well everywhere.

Our prices and timelines

At OakFox, brand identity projects run from £4,500 for a focused visual refresh to £18,000+ for a full identity system with naming, messaging, guidelines and launch assets.

  • A focused refresh typically takes four to six weeks.
  • A full identity system usually runs eight to twelve weeks from kick-off to handover.

We quote each project after a free call rather than selling fixed packages, because two businesses asking for “a rebrand” rarely need the same thing.

What moves the price

Strategy depth. A business with a clear position needs less discovery than one still working out who it’s for. Research, workshops and stakeholder interviews take time, and they’re usually worth it.

Naming. Finding a name that’s distinctive, available and trademarkable is a project in its own right. If you already have a name you’re keeping, that part drops away.

How many places the brand has to work. A brand that lives on a website and social media is simpler than one that also needs packaging, vehicle livery, signage and print.

Messaging and voice. Some projects stop at the visual system. Others include a messaging framework and tone-of-voice guide, which makes the website copy, the sales deck and the social posts much easier to write afterwards.

Guideline depth. A few pages of essentials, or a full guide covering every application? The answer depends on how many people, and suppliers, will be using the brand without you in the room.

Refresh or rebrand?

Not every business that feels tired needs a rebrand. If people already recognise you and your positioning still holds, a refresh, modernising the visual system while keeping what’s familiar, is usually the better investment. Recognition is expensive to build, and a full rebrand throws some of it away.

A full rebrand makes sense when the business itself has changed: a new audience, a new offer, a merger, or a name that no longer fits. If sustainability is part of the reason for the change, read rebranding without greenwashing before you announce anything. UK regulators now expect environmental claims to be backed up.

Getting value from the budget

A few things make any branding budget go further:

  1. Brief well. A clear brief saves weeks of exploration. Our guide on how to brief a branding agency covers the eight things every brief should include.
  2. Decide who signs off. Projects slow down and costs rise when new decision-makers appear at the final presentation.
  3. Plan the launch. A new identity only pays off once it’s applied. Budget time, or money, for updating the website, social profiles and printed materials.
  4. Build for longevity. A brand that lasts ten years costs less per year than one you redo in three. That’s also the most sustainable choice, as we argue in what makes a brand sustainable.

What's the difference between a logo and a brand identity?
