In July we measured 99 UK small-business websites for carbon. In November we went back to the same sites with a different question: how many of them can everyone actually use?

## The Question

How accessible is the ordinary UK small-business website, what goes wrong most often, and are the heavy sites also the inaccessible ones?

## The Method

We rechecked the sites from our [carbon study](/portfolio/uk-sme-carbon-study/): independent plumbers, dentists, solicitors and salons found through ordinary searches. 96 of the 99 could still be reached. Each homepage, plus up to two inner pages, went through [A11yMate](/portfolio/a11ymate/), the accessibility checker we build, using the same engine as its free public page checker: 41 checks on the code and 10 in a browser, against WCAG 2.2 levels A and AA.

We counted only what the checker can decide without a person, discarded contrast measurements it could not make reliably, and checked a sample of the rest by eye.

## What We Found

- **Every homepage failed at least one check.** 95 of 96 had a critical or serious problem, and the median homepage had 19 distinct problems.
- **Low-contrast text is the most common failure**, on about three in four homepages. Missing skip links (68%) and skipped heading levels (63%) follow.
- **Half of all homepages have links with no readable text**, mostly icons for phone, social media and baskets.
- **A light site is not an accessible site.** The lightest third of sites had a median of 19.5 problems and the heaviest third had 19.

The full analysis, including the sector table, is in the write-up: [We Checked 96 UK Small Business Websites for Accessibility](/blog/we-checked-uk-small-business-websites-for-accessibility/).

## The Dataset

Anonymised per-site results are published under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) as [CSV](https://oakfox.co.uk/data/oakfox-uk-sme-accessibility-study-2026-11.csv) and [JSON aggregates](https://oakfox.co.uk/data/oakfox-uk-sme-accessibility-study-2026-11-aggregates.json).

## Why It Matters to Clients

Automated checks are a floor, not a verdict, and this study says so. But the problems it found are common, well understood and usually quick to fix. It is the evidence behind how we scope [accessibility work](/services/accessibility/): measure first, fix the known failures, then test the parts no tool can judge.
