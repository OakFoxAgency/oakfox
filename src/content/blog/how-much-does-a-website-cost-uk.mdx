A website in the UK costs from about £2 a month on a DIY builder to £2,500 to £20,000+ for a custom build by a studio like ours. Builder plans run from £1.99 to £259 a month, freelancers charge roughly £25 to £50 an hour and small agencies £50 to £150 an hour ([Startups.co.uk, updated March 2026](https://www.startups.co.uk/web-design/how-much-does-a-website-cost/)). For a small business, the figure that matters is the total: the build, a domain at about £8 to £20 a year, hosting and changes after launch. Below are the ranges by route, our own published prices and what moves a quote up or down.

## How much does a website cost in the UK?

| Route | Upfront cost | Ongoing cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY website builder** | Little or nothing | £1.99 to £259 a month, plus your time | Testing an idea, very simple sites |
| **Freelancer** | Charged by the hour, about £25 to £50 | Hosting, plus paid changes | Small, well-defined sites on a tight budget |
| **Independent studio** (e.g. OakFox) | Design from £3,500; custom build from £2,500 | Hosting from £9.99/month, plus optional support | Businesses whose website needs to win work |
| **Agency** | Charged by the hour, about £50 to £150 and up | Retainers are common | Complex sites, big teams, many stakeholders |

Builder, freelancer and agency figures are from Startups.co.uk (March 2026, fetched October 2026); the OakFox row is from our [web design](/services/web-design/) and [development](/services/development/) pages.

The honest summary: you're not buying pages, you're buying decisions. A cheaper quote usually means fewer decisions made on your behalf: about structure, content, speed, accessibility, and how the site will be run in three years' time.

## How much does a website cost for a small business?

For a sole trader or small firm with a brochure site, the sensible options are a builder plan or a focused studio build. A builder keeps the upfront cost near zero but you do the design, writing and upkeep yourself. A studio build from around £2,500 (static marketing site) or £3,500 (a focused landing-page design) is a one-off cost that buys structure, copy direction, speed and accessibility. Most of our small-business projects need design and build together, and we quote them together after a free call.

## What actually moves the price

When two quotes for "a five-page website" differ by thousands, it's almost always one of these.

**Templates, not pages.** A site with twenty pages that share three layouts is cheaper to design than a site with eight pages that all look different. Ask a studio how many *templates* they're quoting for; it's the more honest unit.

**Who writes the content.** Content is the most underestimated line on any website project. If you're supplying words and photos, say so and set a date. If not, budget for copy. Sites stall more often waiting for content than for design.

**Integrations.** Online booking, payments, e-commerce, a members' area, a CRM connection: each one adds design states, build time and testing. A brochure site and a site that takes bookings are different projects.

**Standards.** Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA, a performance budget, verified green hosting. These are part of how we build by default, not extras, but they're exactly what a very low quote leaves out. [Green web design costs roughly nothing extra](/blog/what-is-green-web-design-and-cost/) when it's done as part of the build; retrofitting it later costs far more.

**Brand readiness.** If you don't yet have a logo, colours and a clear sense of voice, some of that gets decided during the website project, or needs a [branding project](/services/branding/) first. Better to know which before the quote than after.

## Website costs per year: the running costs nobody quotes

The build is only part of the bill. Plan for:

- **Domain renewal**, once a year: roughly £8 to £20 for a domain name ([Startups.co.uk, March 2026](https://www.startups.co.uk/web-design/how-much-does-a-website-cost/)).
- **Hosting.** Our [managed green hosting](/services/green-web-hosting/) has three plans with free migration, on infrastructure verified by The Green Web Foundation: Starter at **£9.99 a month** for a single site, Growth at **£22.99 a month** for up to three sites with priority support and a quarterly check-in, and Business at **£45.99 a month** for up to ten sites with hands-on involvement. Every plan covers daily backups kept 14 days, free SSL and UK email support.
- **Updates and changes.** Either your time (if the site has a CMS you're comfortable with) or paid time from whoever built it. Our [website care plan](/services/care-plan/) covers hosting, updates, a monthly report and an hour of edits from £79 a month.
- **Plugins and licences**, if the site runs on WordPress: premium plugins, themes and the security updates that come with them.

Lighter sites are cheaper to run. A static-first site has fewer moving parts to patch, needs less hosting, and loads faster for visitors, which is why we build that way when the project allows.

## Why the cheapest quote often costs more

We [audited 100 UK small-business homepages](/blog/we-audited-100-uk-small-business-websites/) earlier this year. The median page transferred 1.8 MB, and 44 of the 99 sites we could measure shipped over a megabyte of images on the homepage alone. That weight is what makes a site feel slow on a phone, and it's rarely a deliberate choice: it's what happens when nobody on the project was responsible for it.

The same pattern shows up in accessibility, in content that was never finished, and in sites nobody can edit without calling a developer. None of these appear on a quote. All of them turn up later, usually as a rebuild two or three years earlier than it should have been.

This isn't an argument for spending as much as possible. A focused, well-made site with five pages will outperform a sprawling one with thirty. It's an argument for knowing what's in the quote.

## How much should a website cost? Get a quote you can trust

Before you ask anyone for a price, write down:

1. **What the site is for:** enquiries, bookings, sales, credibility. Pick the one that matters most.
2. **Roughly how many distinct page types** you need, and any must-have features.
3. **Who's providing the content**, and when.
4. **What you already have:** brand, photography, an existing site to migrate.
5. **Your budget range.** Sharing it isn't a weakness; it lets a good studio tell you what's realistic, and a bad fit say so early.

Then ask each supplier the same questions: how many templates is this, what's included for accessibility and performance, who hosts it, and what does it cost to run after launch. The answers will tell you more than the headline figures.
