A logo in the UK costs from £30 to £150 on a marketplace, £500 to £1,000 from a freelancer, £1,000 to £2,000 from a small agency and £2,000 to £3,500 from a mid-size agency. Those ranges come from Whito's UK benchmark data (June 2026), and a full brand identity from a specialist agency runs to £5,000 to £20,000 on the same page ([Whito, branding design costs UK, page updated November 2026, fetched 20 November 2026](https://whito.co.uk/research/branding-design-costs-uk/)). The difference between the cheap end and the expensive end is mostly strategy, not drawing.

## What a logo costs, by who makes it

| Who makes it | Typical price | What you usually get |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace or DIY maker | £30 to £150 | A file, little or no strategy, a design that may not be unique |
| Freelance designer | £500 to £1,000 | A custom mark, a few concepts, revisions |
| Small agency | £1,000 to £2,000 | A custom mark with some research and a basic rules sheet |
| Mid-size agency | £2,000 to £3,500 | Strategy, several concepts and a fuller set of assets |

Source for the table: [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/branding-design-costs-uk/), fetched 20 November 2026. These are one consultancy's benchmark figures, not a survey of every designer, so treat them as a guide to the shape of the market.

Other published figures agree on the middle and disagree at the edges. Canny Creative, an agency, says a well-designed professional logo costs £500 to £1,000, that freelancers range from £150 to £5,000, and that its own minimum is £1,000 ([Canny Creative, published February 2018, no update date shown](https://www.canny-creative.com/blog/how-much-does-logo-design-cost/)). Airtasker's UK page gives a median of £540, a low of £10 and a high of £2,500, with designer hourly rates of £20 to £150 ([Airtasker, last updated November 2023](https://www.airtasker.com/uk/costs/logo-design/logo-designer-cost)). Both are older than the Whito figures, so the newer ranges are the safer guide.

## Why logo prices vary so much

Three things move the number.

1. **Strategy.** A logo that starts with who your customers are and what you do differently takes more time than one drawn from a brief of "something clean". That time is the cost.
2. **Concepts and rounds.** One concept and one revision is cheaper than three concepts and unlimited changes.
3. **What you receive.** A single PNG is not the same as vector files, colour and single-colour versions, a favicon and the right to own the work.

A hidden cost sits outside the price: a logo that looks like a thousand others is hard to protect and easy to forget. If you plan to trademark it, ask the designer how they check for similar marks.

## Questions to ask before you pay anyone

A quote is easier to compare once you know what sits behind it. Put these to every designer or studio on your shortlist.

- **How many concepts will I see, and how many rounds of changes are included?** Some prices cover one idea and one revision. Others cover several. Neither is wrong, but they are not the same product.
- **Which files do I get?** You want vector files that scale to a shop sign or a business card, a version for dark backgrounds and a single-colour version. A flattened image is not enough.
- **Who owns the design afterwards?** You should own the final artwork outright. Check this is written down.
- **Are the fonts licensed for my use?** A logo set in a font you are not licensed to use can cause trouble later, especially in print and on a website.
- **How will you check the mark is not too close to someone else's?** A good designer has an answer, even if it is a search of the register and of competitors rather than a legal opinion.
- **What happens if I want to change the colour or add a strapline later?** Straightforward changes should not mean a new project.

A designer who answers these plainly is a better sign than a lower price. If you cannot get a straight answer, keep looking.

## When a logo refresh is enough

If your business already has a recognisable mark that customers know, you may only need it tidied: redrawn cleanly, a better colour set, and files that work on a screen. That is usually cheaper than a new logo, and it keeps the recognition you have built. A full rebrand makes sense when the business has changed, when the name is changing or when the old mark cannot be fixed. Our [guide to branding costs](/blog/how-much-does-branding-cost-uk/) explains how to tell the difference.

## How to spend less and still get a good logo

- **Brief it properly.** A clear brief saves rounds. Our guide to [briefing a branding agency](/blog/how-to-brief-a-branding-agency/) covers what to write down.
- **Choose the name first.** A logo for a name you later change is money spent twice; see [choosing a brand name](/blog/choosing-a-brand-name/).
- **Ask what is included before you ask the price.** Files, concepts, rounds, ownership.
- **Check the work, not the price.** Look at whether a designer's earlier logos look different from each other. If they all look alike, so will yours.

## The short version

For a business that will trade for years, budget £500 to £1,000 for a freelancer logo, or from £4,500 for an identity where the logo is one part of a system. For a side project, a marketplace logo at £30 to £150 is fine. Anything under £500 from a person is worth asking questions about, and anything over £3,500 for a logo alone should come with real strategy.
